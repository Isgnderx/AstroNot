Forced Spectrophotometry

Measure SPHEREx spectra by forced ePSF photometry on every spectral image covering a position, read directly from the IRSA archive — the IRSA Spectrophotometry Tool's method, in your browser.

For aperture photometry of calibrated images you already have, use Spectrophotometry.

Frames

Import one or more IRSA SIA result files (CSV) saved from Observation Search: every SPHEREx level 2 image in them is a frame, read from the IRSA archive copy on Amazon S3 a few kilobytes at a time. You can also choose SPHEREx level 2 FITS files on this device; a file needs its EPSF extension (QR3, DR1 and later) to be measured. A frame listed twice is measured once.

Targets

Up to 20 sky positions (ICRS). Every target is fitted on every frame it falls on; targets close together are fitted jointly. A target is a point source unless you switch on its Galaxy model (a Sérsic profile convolved with the ePSF; an effective radius below 1 arcsec is treated as a point source, as IRSA does). A targets CSV has the columns ra, dec and optionally name, sersic_n, re_arcsec, axis_ratio, pa_deg, in degrees.

Galaxy model fluxes are indicative only.

    Filters and parameters

    Filters choose which loaded frames are measured (MJD is the frame start time) and which spectral points are kept (wavelength in μm). Leave a bound empty for no limit. The defaults are spxphot's.

    Detectors
    Advanced

    Fit size and background half-size take whole numbers from 1 to 99. That bound is this page's, not spxphot's: spxphot accepts any fit size, fractional ones included, and any background half-size. Concurrent frames is how many frames are read at once (1 to 12, 6 when the field is empty); spxphot reads 48 at once by default.

    How the flux is computed

    This page is a port of spxphot, an independent Rust reimplementation of the IRSA SPHEREx Spectrophotometry Tool, and follows its method step by step. Each frame that covers a target gives one spectral point:

    1. Find frames. spxphot asks IRSA TAP for them. TAP sends no CORS header, so a browser cannot ask it; here the frame list comes from the SIA search that Observation Search builds (for BD+17 4708 the same 352 frames: DR1 239, QR3 113), filtered by deep fields, detector and MJD.
    2. Read only the needed bytes. HTTP range requests to IRSA's public copy of the archive on Amazon S3, which allows reads from other sites: about 25 rows of IMAGE and VARIANCE around the target, the same rows of the RICE_1-compressed FLAGS image (decompressed here), and the ePSF and WCS-WAVE tables. The two tables are kept per calibration and downloaded once per detector, not once per frame.
    3. Position. TAN-SIP sky to pixel: an inverse-SIP first guess, then Newton iteration on the forward model, which matches astropy's all_world2pix to better than 1e-6 arcsec.
    4. PSF. The ePSF zone nearest the target, evaluated at the pixel centres with a not-a-knot bicubic spline (the interpolant of photutils ImagePSF) and normalised to a sum of 1. For a galaxy model the analytic Sérsic profile is sampled on the ePSF's 1/5-pixel lattice and convolved with the ePSF; the local WCS Jacobian turns pixels into east and north arcsec.
    5. Fit. Pixels whose flags are on IRSA's ignore list are masked. In a circle 13 px across, the fluxes and a background are solved by weighted linear least squares, data = Σ Fₖ·Pₖ + B with weights 1/VARIANCE. Positions and shapes are fixed, so the model is linear and one solve gives the exact optimum (Tractor, IRSA's engine, reaches it iteratively). Targets close together are fitted jointly.
    6. Units. flux [μJy] = F [MJy/sr per pixel] × Ω [sr] × 10¹², with the pixel solid angle Ω from the WCS Jacobian and the gnomonic area factor (1 + ρ²)^−3/2. flux_err adds a 3 % systematic floor in quadrature to the fit error (flux_err_tractor), as IRSA does.

    Quality. fit_ql is the mean of |data − model|/σ within 2.5 px of the target. flags is the OR of the FLAGS pixels within 2.5 px, plus bit 32 CONTAINS_BAD_PIXEL (any flag other than SOURCE and OUTLIER within 2.5 px) and bit 33 FIT_ERROR (the fit has no solution), as IRSA sets them. Wavelength and bandwidth are interpolated bilinearly in the WCS-WAVE table.

    This port against spxphot. On the test cutout every column equals spxphot's own output to 1e-9 relative (fit_ql to 1e-6) with identical flags, and a full run of the BD+17 4708 example in the browser matched spxphot's CSV on 352 of 352 rows (largest relative difference 6.4e-12).

    Known differences from the IRSA tool. The wavelength differs by a median of +0.0003 μm (at most 0.002 μm, about 12 % of one channel's bandwidth): IRSA evaluates the spectral WCS slightly differently from a plain WCS-WAVE lookup. fit_ql runs about 6 % higher than IRSA's although the fluxes agree. About 2 % of the bright-star points differ by more than 3σ, most on frames with near-saturation flags or SOURCE-flagged neighbours, where IRSA's exact masking rule is unknown. The 13 px circle and the joint constant sky were calibrated against IRSA output, not read from IRSA's code, which is not public.

    Galaxy model: not validated. With a Sérsic profile spxphot's fluxes ran about 4 % above IRSA's (NGC 7619, median ratio 1.040) with a different spectral shape. A likely but unconfirmed cause is that Tractor approximates Sérsic profiles with a mixture of Gaussians, while this method uses the exact profile. Treat galaxy fluxes as indicative only.
