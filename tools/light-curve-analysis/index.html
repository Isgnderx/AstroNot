Light Curve Analysis

Visualize how a source’s measured brightness changes over time.

Time-series file

CSV format

Open a CSV file of measurements of one or more sources at different times. The primary format is the Spectrophotometry CSV (source_id, obs_id, mjd, ra, dec, wavelength, lambda_width, flux, flux_err, flags, method, with MJD in UTC, wavelength in µm and flux in µJy). Any other table works too: you choose the time column, its format (ISO 8601, JD or MJD) and time scale, and the measurement kind (flux or magnitude) and unit. Files are read on this device and are never uploaded.

        How times and values are handled

        Time. ISO 8601, JD and MJD are read as given. Inside one time scale JD = MJD + 2400000.5 exactly. UTC, TAI and TT convert exactly: TAI = UTC + the integer leap-second offset (IERS list, 1972 to 2027-06-28, 37 s since 2017) and TT = TAI + 32.184 s. TDB differs from TT by a position-dependent term of about 1.7 ms, so a TDB time is only compared with TDB; the same holds for an unknown scale. A time is labeled BJD only when you state that the file is barycentre-corrected, and it is only compared with other barycentric times. Rows whose time cannot be put on the plot’s scale are left out and counted.

        Flux and magnitude. Flux densities in Jy, mJy, µJy and nJy convert exactly. AB magnitudes use m = −2.5 log₁₀(F / 3631 Jy) and the uncertainty σₘ = 1.0857 · σ_F / F (first order, valid when σ_F is much smaller than F). A flux of zero or less has no magnitude, so it stays in the table but is not plotted. Vega and unspecified magnitudes are never converted. On a magnitude axis smaller numbers are brighter and are drawn at the top.

        Series and statistics. A series is one source in one band. A band is a label from the file or a wavelength bin you define; unbinned data have no defined band and get no mean or median. Missing measurements, negative or zero flux and unknown uncertainties stay in the table and are never replaced by invented values. SPHEREx observes one sky position at a different wavelength in each exposure, so a wavelength bin that is wide mixes spectral shape with change over time.
