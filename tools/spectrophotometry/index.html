Spectrophotometry

Measure the flux density of one sky position in calibrated SPHEREx images and write a spectrum that Spectrum Lab can open.

For the IRSA forced-photometry method (an ePSF fit on every frame covering a position, read from the archive), use Forced Spectrophotometry.

Calibrated images

Choose one or more SPHEREx Level 2 FITS images or cutouts (uncompressed, IMAGE in MJy/sr, TAN or TAN-SIP WCS in ICRS). The tool also reads a FLAGS image, a VARIANCE image and the WCS-WAVE table when the file has them. Each file is one exposure, so each gives one point of the spectrum. Files are read on this device.

Target and aperture

Enter the sky position in decimal degrees (ICRS). The position is mapped to a pixel of every image with that image's own WCS. The aperture is a circle around it; the background comes from the annulus between the two outer radii. Changing any value clears the results until you measure again.

    How the flux is computed

    Aperture flux density = 10⁶ × Σ wᵢ (Iᵢ − b) Ωᵢ in Jy, with Iᵢ and the background b in MJy/sr and Ωᵢ the solid angle of pixel i in sr. The weights wᵢ are the exact overlaps of the pixels with the circle (partly covered pixels count by their covered fraction). Ωᵢ comes from the image WCS (the sky area of the pixel corners), not from a constant. The result is shown in μJy.

    Background b is the sigma-clipped median (or mean) of the valid annulus pixels, with 3σ clipping. Pixels that are NaN, BLANK or flagged (SUR_ERROR, NONFUNC, MISSING_DATA, HOT, COLD, NONLINEAR, PERSIST, BLOOM, SNOWBALL, HALO, SATELLITE_HALO, the bits the archive tool ignores when fitting) are left out of the aperture and the annulus, never replaced. The flux is not corrected for left-out area; the covered share and the masked share are shown, and a measurement below the minimum coverage is refused. A target outside the image is not measured.

    Wavelength is read from the per-pixel spectral WCS of the file (the WCS-WAVE lookup table) at the target pixel. A file without that table gives a flux but no spectrum point, and is left out of the CSV. Uncertainty is propagated from the VARIANCE image only (independent pixel noise plus the error of the background level); without a VARIANCE image the uncertainty is empty. It contains no calibration, aperture-correction or correlated-noise term. Flags are the bitwise OR of the FLAGS values of the aperture pixels, plus bit 32 (CONTAINS_BAD_PIXEL) when a pixel was left out, as in the IRSA table.

    Not the archive's measurement. The IRSA SPHEREx Spectrophotometry tool fits a PSF and morphology model with Tractor (forced photometry); this tool sums pixels in a circle. The two differ for blended or extended sources, and no aperture correction is applied here, so compare them only with that difference in mind. The time (mjd) is the mid-exposure time (MJD-AVG) in UTC when the header has it, otherwise the exposure start.
