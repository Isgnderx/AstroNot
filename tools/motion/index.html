Motion Detector

Track position changes in asteroids, comets, and nearby stars across epochs.

Observations of one field

This tool finds candidates. It does not confirm a moving object. Load two or more FITS observations of the same field (three or more are better: they give a fitted rate and its residuals). Files are read on this device. Each must be an uncompressed two-dimensional image with a TAN or TAN-SIP WCS in ICRS and a known BUNIT, and all must use the same units. One observation is the reference; every other one is subtracted from it, Δ = epoch − reference, and the tool pairs a negative residual (where the reference has flux the epoch lacks) with a positive one (where the epoch has flux the reference lacks). Residual pairs also come from variable stars, imperfect alignment, PSF and background differences and artifacts, so every candidate needs a look at the original images.

      Export

      Download every candidate as CSV: one row per position with the candidate ID, review status, observation ID, file name and HDU, observation time (UTC, MJD and its source), reference-grid pixel, ICRS RA and Dec, the pixel in the epoch's own file, east and north offsets, fit residual, displacement, rate (arcsec/hour and arcsec/day), position angle, the reason a rate is empty, the difference direction and the detection method. Empty cells mean unavailable. Dismissed candidates are included with their status.

      How candidates are found, and what they are not

      Δ = epoch − reference is computed by the Image Differencing code for pixels valid in both images, after the units, the sky overlap and the pixel grid are checked from the WCS (equal array sizes alone are never enough). Epochs on another grid are reprojected onto the reference grid by bilinear interpolation, which smooths them and correlates their noise. No PSF matching or background correction is applied.

      Residuals. Local maxima and minima of Δ above the chosen threshold are listed. The threshold method is named in the result. Peaks within two pixels of the image edge, of a NaN or masked pixel or of the area the epoch does not cover are not examined. A positive and a negative residual closer than the search radius and farther apart than the minimum separation form a pair. A pair is dropped when both original images are bright at its midpoint (a shifted static star) and unpaired residuals (brightness changes, transients) are counted but never called moving. Sources that move less than about the minimum separation, or whose lobes overlap a bright neighbour, are not found.

      Positions and rates. A candidate keeps one position per epoch on the reference grid, measured as a centroid in the original image (or marked by you). RA and Dec come from the reference WCS, the position in each epoch's file from that epoch's WCS. Displacement is the great-circle separation in arcsec and the position angle is measured from north through east. Two positions give an end-to-end rate; three or more give a straight-line least-squares fit in east and north against time, with residuals and a formal rate uncertainty. Position errors are not propagated, and motion that is not along a straight line at constant speed is not modelled. If a time is missing or two times are equal, the rate is left empty and the reason is shown; you can type a UTC time for an observation whose header has none, and it is labelled as entered manually.
