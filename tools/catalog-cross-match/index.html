Catalog Cross-Match

Compare SPHEREx source positions with entries in Gaia and WISE.

Match your positions with a catalog extract

Load your sources (a CSV with an ID, RA and Dec) and a catalog extract (a CSV saved from the archive, such as Gaia DR3 or AllWISE). Both files are read on this device; nothing is uploaded. For every source of yours, AstroNot lists the catalog rows within the radius you set, nearest first. A match here is a position match only; it does not confirm that two entries are the same object.

2. Catalog extract

CSV format

A CSV saved from an archive cone search. Gaia (source_id, ra, dec, pmra, pmdec, ref_epoch, parallax) and AllWISE (designation, ra, dec, pmra, pmdec, w1mjdmean, …) tables from IRSA are recognised. Catalog IDs are kept as text, so 19-digit Gaia IDs stay exact. Lines at the top that start with # (for example # release: DR3) are read as file information.

3. Match settings

The default radius of 3 arcsec is a starting value, not a recommendation for your data: choose it from the position uncertainty of your sources and of the catalog. A catalog row is a candidate when its separation is at most the radius (the boundary is included). Radius range: 0.01 to 3600 arcsec.

    The CSV has one row for each candidate (rank 1 is the nearest) and one row for every source with no candidate or with invalid coordinates. It keeps IDs as text, positions in degrees, separations in arcsec, the radius, the comparison epoch and each catalog row's published epoch, and the catalog name and release.

    How the match works

    Separation is the great-circle angle between two positions, computed with Vincenty's formula, which stays accurate from sub-milliarcsecond to antipodal distances. RA is handled across 0°/360° (359.9999° and 0.0001° on the equator are 0.72 arcsec apart) and near the poles, where every RA is searched. Positions are read as ICRS / J2000 degrees; a catalog in another frame or equinox must be converted first.

    Candidates are all catalog rows with a separation of at most the radius. They are sorted by separation. Separations that agree within 0.001 milliarcsec count as a tie, and a tie is broken by catalog ID (digit-only IDs in numeric order) and then by catalog row, so the order never depends on file order. Nearest candidate is simply the first of that list. It is a position match only; whether two entries are the same object needs more evidence, such as brightness, colour, proper motion or the position uncertainties.

    Epochs. Without proper motion, separations compare your positions as given with the catalog positions at their published epoch. If you move the catalog to an epoch, each row is carried along a great circle by its proper motion for the time between its own epoch and the chosen one; parallax and radial velocity are ignored. Gaia pmra is μα* = μα·cos(Dec), already including cos(Dec) (Gaia DR3 documentation); the AllWISE documentation does not say, so you choose. Rows without proper motion or epoch keep their published position and are counted. Your own positions are never moved.

    Coverage. A source with no match may simply lie outside the extract. If you give the extract's centre and radius, AstroNot marks sources whose whole search circle is not inside it; if the number of rows equals the request's row limit, the extract may be cut off and coverage is unknown.

    The example holds 40 AllWISE Source Catalog rows (allwise_p3as_psd) and 72 Gaia DR3 rows (gaia_dr3_source) returned by NASA/IPAC IRSA's TAP service on 13 Nov 2026 for a 90 arcsec cone around RA 156.09328159°, Dec −41.64466331° (the SPHEREx example position). AllWISE rows stand in for a list of your own sources; no SPHEREx source list is bundled.
